### Full Video Script — AFib Explainer

Your heart has its own electrical system — like a conductor leading an orchestra.

In a healthy heart, the conductor sends a steady beat, about 60 to 100 times a minute. Every chamber contracts in perfect order.

In atrial fibrillation, the upper chambers start firing chaotic electrical signals — like dozens of conductors all waving at once. Instead of a steady beat, your heart quivers and beats irregularly.

This matters for two big reasons.

First, when the upper chambers quiver instead of pumping, blood can pool there. Pooled blood can form clots. If a clot travels to your brain, it causes a stroke. That is why your doctor prescribed a blood thinner — it is your most important protection.

Second, AFib can make your heart beat too fast, which tires the heart muscle over time. Rate control medications keep that in check.

The good news: most people with AFib live full, active lives.

With the right medications, regular monitoring through your connected device, and understanding your personal triggers, you can manage this condition confidently.

Your care team at HelloAlfred monitors your readings between visits so nothing gets missed.
