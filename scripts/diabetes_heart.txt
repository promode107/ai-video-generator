### Full Video Script — Diabetes and Your Heart

If you have diabetes, your heart needs extra attention.

Here is why. When blood sugar stays high over time, it damages the walls of your blood vessels. Damaged blood vessels become stiff and narrow, making it harder for blood to flow. This significantly raises your risk of heart attack and stroke.

In fact, people with diabetes are two to four times more likely to develop heart disease than people without diabetes.

But the connection goes both ways — taking care of your heart also helps your diabetes.

There are three numbers your care team watches closely.

Your blood sugar, measured as HbA1c, shows your average glucose over the past three months. Keeping it below seven percent reduces damage to your blood vessels.

Your blood pressure should stay below 130 over 80. High blood pressure is especially harmful to already-stressed blood vessels.

Your cholesterol — specifically LDL — should be kept low, ideally below 70 if you already have heart disease. Statins are often prescribed to help with this.

Your medications work together as a team. Some of your diabetes medications, like SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 agonists, also directly protect your heart and kidneys — so taking them every day matters more than you might realize.

HelloAlfred helps you track all three numbers in one place. Your care team monitors your readings and can reach out when something needs attention — before it becomes a problem.
